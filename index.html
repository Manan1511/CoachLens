Why we built it

Elite academies measure bowling actions in camera labs that cost more than most clubs earn in a decade. Everyone else works from memory, a phone held at an odd angle, and a coach's eye at the end of a long session. CoachLens gives grassroots coaches the same thing the labs have — consistent numbers on a bowler's own action, session after session — using a phone they already own.

How it works

Four steps, one net session

01
Step 01

Set the camera down

Any phone on a tripod at hip height, about three metres side-on to the crease, lens up against the net. Our framing guide gets you there in under thirty seconds — and the same position every session is what makes the numbers comparable.

02
Step 02

Film the spell

Record with the camera app already on the phone — nothing to install, nothing to sign into at the nets. Shoot at the highest frame rate it offers and let the session run.

03
Step 03

Upload to CoachLens

Drop the clips into your browser, from the pavilion or from home. We check each one is usable before analysing it — steady frame rate, no motion blur, bowler clearly visible. Unusable clips get flagged, not guessed at.

04
Step 04

Review and decide

Each delivery comes back with two numbers, how they compare to that bowler's baseline, and a plain-language note on what changed. Assign a drill, dismiss the flag, or just log it — then share a clean summary of the numbers without sharing the video.

What we measure

Two things, measured properly

We'd rather do two measurements well than twenty badly. Both are taken from a single side-on view, and both come with their working shown.

Front knee at landing

Whether the front leg stays braced as a lever or collapses into the ground at front foot contact. It's one of the clearest markers of how a bowler is transferring effort — and one of the first things to shift when they tire.

Trunk lean at release

How far forward the upper body is at the moment of release. It shapes release height and trajectory, and a steady drift across a spell is often the first visible sign of fatigue setting in.
